Overview

Chain Reaction — A Domino Effect Challenge. In this challenge, we hand each team an existing startup, presented under a fictional name, and ask them to run it with limited resources. Each decision creates consequences: a choice may unlock growth, cause a setback, or force a pivot. We expect teams to explain their decisions, respond to surprise market events, and pitch the strategy they would take forward.

Core message

Entrepreneurship is a chain of decisions. One move can change everything.

The startup: a real business under a fictional name

The startup doesn't have to be invented. We can base the brief on an original, existing startup and present it under a fictional name. This gives teams clear boundaries for where they work, instead of an open blank page.

What is fixed for every team (the boundaries):

  • Industry and product: what the startup sells and the problem it solves.
  • Stage: for example, post-launch with early revenue, so teams know whether to build, sell, or scale.
  • Target customer and market: who the startup serves and where.
  • Resources: the starting budget, team size, and capacity limits stated in the brief.
  • Constraints: the competitors, suppliers, and costs that exist in the startup's world.

What teams control:

The decisions. Where to spend the budget, which customers to prioritise, whom to hire, and when to pivot, all inside those boundaries. Teams cannot change what the startup is; they decide what it does next.

Ground rules for using a real startup:

  • We will use only public information, or details the founders have approved.
  • We will replace the real name and any identifying details, such as brand, logo, and founders' names.
  • We will give every team the same brief so the comparison stays fair.
1

Event

Format

Team-based startup simulation, closing with the Ripple Pitch: each team's three-minute story of how its first move set off a chain of consequences.

Goal

Make thoughtful decisions, manage limited resources, adapt to changing conditions, and build a viable plan.

Suggested team task

Each team receives the same startup brief (an existing startup under a fictional name), a starting budget, and decision options. After each round, consequences are revealed.

Outcome

Teams present the decisions they made, what changed, and what they would do next.

2

Event Flow — Full Run of Show

  1. A

    Pre-event setup (before attendees enter)

    • We will set up the entrance or the area beside the stage with oversized, lightweight domino props labelled IDEA, RISK, FAILURE, DECISION, OPPORTUNITY, GROWTH, and IMPACT. Each prop will be anchored firmly so it cannot topple into a walkway.

    Make or buy? We decided on making them in-house and buying only the supplies. Oversized lightweight dominoes are rarely sold ready-made in the size we need, so buying would mean custom orders. Building them ourselves fits our DIY budget of ₹4,000–11,000 and the 2–3 week preparation window, and our IEDC members can paint and label them in E-Summit branding and control their weight and stability.

    Buy

    Foam board or cardboard sheets, paint, adhesive, stands or supports, and any printed backdrop or labels.

    Make

    Cutting, painting, labelling, and assembling the dominoes, including the final IMPACT domino.

    If time runs short, we will commission a local print shop for foam-board cutouts instead.

    • We will set up registration, team tables, a visible timer, projector, microphone, score desk, and clearly marked exits. Each team station will have its startup brief, decision sheets, budget tokens, and surprise-event cards.
    • We will brief the volunteers, judges, and host, test the domino effect, and prepare a prerecorded video or slide sequence as a backup.
  2. B

    Opening — “The First Domino” 2–3 minutes

    • Dim the stage lights and spotlight one domino. The host says: “Every chain reaction starts somewhere.”
    • A guest or event representative pushes the first domino. Use a safe, controlled physical chain reaction, or play the backup video if the setup is unreliable.
    • Reveal the E-Summit logo and theme: THE DOMINO EFFECT — ONE MOVE CHANGES EVERYTHING. The host links the visual to entrepreneurship: one decision can create consequences far beyond the first move.
  3. C

    Registration and team briefing

    • We will check in attendees, assign team IDs, and direct teams to their tables. The host explains the objective, timing, scoring, and rules.
    • We will tell teams up front that there may not be one perfect answer: judges will score the reasoning behind decisions, not luck alone. Teams must record the decision they make and why.
  4. D

    Round 1 — “The First Domino”

    Every team gets the same startup pack, and it has four parts.

    Startup scenario

    What it means

    Who the startup is: its fictional name, what it sells, its stage, and its team size. It is the real startup's profile with identifying details removed.

    Example

    “NovaCart”, a 12-month-old grocery delivery app run by a team of five

    Target customer

    What it means

    The specific group the startup serves, what they need, and what they use today instead.

    Example

    Working professionals in one city who want groceries within 30 minutes

    Starting budget

    What it means

    The virtual money each team begins with, given as budget tokens. Every option on the decision sheet has a fixed token cost, and unspent tokens carry forward.

    Example

    The same amount for all teams, for example ₹10 lakh in tokens

    Initial challenge

    What it means

    The first problem that forces a decision, with a number attached so teams can reason from evidence.

    Example

    Many downloads, but few customers order a second time

    Teams then choose their first move from four options. Each option costs tokens, works at a different speed, and carries a different risk.

    Product development

    What it meansSpend on building or improving the product: new features, fixing defects, or a better prototype.

    Possible upsideA stronger product that keeps customers and supports higher prices.

    Possible riskHigh cost, slow results, and the team may build something customers do not want.

    Marketing

    What it meansSpend on reaching customers: ads, promotions, social media, or local outreach.

    Possible upsideFast visibility and new customers.

    Possible riskMoney goes quickly, and a weak product or message wastes it.

    Customer research

    What it meansSpend on talking to customers through surveys, interviews, and tests to learn what they need and will pay for.

    Possible upsideClear evidence for later decisions, at low cost.

    Possible riskNo immediate growth, and competitors may move first.

    Hiring

    What it meansSpend on adding people, such as a developer, salesperson, or operations hire.

    Possible upsideMore capacity to build, sell, or deliver.

    Possible riskSalaries keep reducing the budget in every later round.

    • Teams submit the choice and a short reason before the deadline. The reason is one or two sentences naming the logic or evidence behind the choice.
    • The coordinator reveals the immediate consequence and updates each team's budget or progress sheet.
  5. E

    Round 2 — “Chain Reaction”

    • Teams make follow-up decisions using the new situation created by Round 1. They must track money, customer response, team capacity, and business risk.
    • The facilitator introduces surprise market-event cards, such as a competitor launching a similar product, a supplier delay, unexpected customer feedback, a sudden cost increase, or a partnership offer.
    • Teams decide how to respond and explain the trade-off. Update scores and resources consistently across teams.
  6. F

    Break and score update

    • Collect decision sheets, update the score tracker, and give teams a short break. Announce only the information needed to keep the game moving; avoid revealing other teams' private strategy sheets.
  7. G

    Round 3 — “The Turning Point”

    For us, the turning point is the moment when the results of Rounds 1 and 2 meet a situation that repeating the same moves cannot solve. We want to see whether a team can read its own evidence, accept a trade-off, and either commit to its path or change course.

    Every team receives one major turning-point scenario, and we will draw the scenarios so that their difficulty is comparable across teams.

    Weak sales

    What happensCustomers try the product, but few buy or return, and cash is running down.

    Decision the team must makeCut costs, fix the product, or change who it sells to?

    Strong demand, low capacity

    What happensOrders exceed what the team can deliver, and delays threaten its reputation.

    Decision the team must makeInvest in growth, hire, seek a partner, or limit orders to protect quality?

    Product failure

    What happensA serious defect or complaint damages customer trust.

    Decision the team must makePause to fix it, communicate openly, or push on with the rest of the plan?

    Chance to pivot

    What happensEarly data shows a different customer segment wants the product more.

    Decision the team must makeStay with the original customer or pivot, and at what cost?

    • Teams choose whether to continue, change direction, cut costs, seek a partner, or invest in growth.
    • They must state what evidence supports the choice and what risk remains. Evidence can come from the team's own sheets: remaining budget, customer response in earlier rounds, and the surprise events it handled.
    • Each team prepares a concise final pitch connecting its first decision to the final outcome.
  8. H

    Final round — “The Ripple Pitch”

    • Each team gives a 3-minute pitch, followed by brief judge questions if time allows.
    • Suggested pitch structure:
      1. our startup and first move
      2. the chain of consequences
      3. the biggest challenge or turning point
      4. how we adapted
      5. our final strategy and next step
    • Judges score teams using the published rubric. A timekeeper gives a 30-second warning and stops pitches at the limit.
  9. I

    Judging, results, and closing

    • Judges compare score sheets and resolve any ties using the published tie-breakers. The host announces winners and recognises strong strategic thinking, adaptability, and teamwork.
    • Close by returning to the theme: every startup is shaped by a series of decisions. Reveal or point to the final domino labelled IMPACT.
    • Optional audience activation: invite attendees to add a small domino card to the Domino Wall answering, “What's one move you've been putting off?” Take approved photos and share a feedback QR code.

Optional audience activations

Domino Wall

Attendees add a small card with an idea, ambition, or one move they want to make.

Chain Reaction Passport

Attendees collect marks for joining sessions, activities, or networking conversations.

One Connection Away

Give attendees simple prompts to meet someone with a complementary skill, idea, or interest.

6

Crowd Management

  • Use separate zones for registration, team tables, stage/pitch area, judge/score desk, and audience seating.
  • Keep entrances, exits, and aisles clear. Secure props and cables; do not run a domino chain across a public walkway.
  • Assign roles: 2 registration volunteers; 3–4 round coordinators; 2 scorekeepers; 2–4 stage/AV and crowd-support volunteers. One person should own the master timeline.
  • Use a visible timer, clear announcements, team labels, and a fixed deadline for collecting every decision sheet.
7

Venue

  • Best option: seminar hall or classroom with movable tables, a projector, microphone, and room for a small stage/installation.
  • An auditorium can work if there is a separate team-work area; otherwise discussion may be difficult.
  • Confirm seating capacity, ventilation, power outlets, accessibility, sound, projector visibility, and permission to install props.
8

Logistics and Props

Theme installation (made in-house):15–25 oversized lightweight domino props; labels/paint; stands or supports; backdrop; one final IMPACT domino.
Game materials:startup briefs, decision sheets, surprise-event cards, virtual-budget tokens or trackers, pens, calculators, team IDs, printed scoring rubrics.
AV and operations:laptop, projector, microphone, timer, spreadsheet or score board, extension cords, tape, signage, and printed backups.
Indicative DIY theme-material budget:₹4,000–₹11,000, excluding venue, AV rental, food, guest travel, and prizes.
9

Miscellaneous

  • We will allow 2–3 weeks of preparation and pilot the game with a small group to check that the scoring, surprise cards, and round timings are understandable.
  • We will publish team size, eligibility, registration deadline, event duration, and what participants should bring.
  • We will prepare contingencies: printed forms, manual scoring, spare scenarios, spare markers/tape, and a prerecorded opening sequence.
  • After the event, we will collect feedback and share only approved photos, and we will keep the closing message short and tied back to the theme.
